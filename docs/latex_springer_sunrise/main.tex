% ABSTRACT
% ===================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Smart hospital campuses rely on localized microgrids to sustain intensive-care ventilators, surgical robotics, and biomedical cold chains.
A stealthy False Data Injection Attack (FDIA) on substation Remote Terminal Units can corrupt the energy-management state estimator while evading its $\chi^{2}$ residual check, placing patient-critical loads at risk.
We introduce a Cognitive Graph Digital Twin (CGDT) that mirrors the hospital electrical topology as a dynamic admittance graph and runs a multi-task Admittance-Conditioned GraphSAGE backbone for simultaneous attack detection and bus-level localization in a single forward pass at 0.908\,ms latency.
A novel Attribution-Guided Multi-Task Loss aligns Integrated-Gradients feature attributions with ground-truth anomaly labels during training, resolving the post-hoc explainability gap and lifting localization F1 by 5.52 percentage points.
The digital twin maintains a formally defined state-space synchronization model with an exponentially weighted rolling baseline.
Upon detecting an attack, a closed-loop healing module quarantines compromised sensor channels and reconstructs measurements via graph-guided spatial interpolation, reducing Weighted Least Squares estimation error by 99.97\%.
On the IEEE 14-bus test feeder under full nonlinear AC power flow, the CGDT achieves $98.25\%\pm0.34\%$ detection F1 and $97.62\%\pm0.84\%$ localization F1 across five random seeds, and retains $92.80\%$ detection F1 under zero-shot topology reconfigurations without retraining.

\keywords{Graph Digital Twin \and Healthcare Cybersecurity \and GraphSAGE \and Explainable AI \and False Data Injection Attacks \and Smart Hospital Microgrid}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The digital transformation of tertiary healthcare facilities has produced cyber-physical ecosystems where uninterrupted electrical supply is not merely an operational convenience but a patient-safety imperative.
Modern smart hospitals operate localized microgrids that integrate utility feeders, rooftop photovoltaic arrays, battery energy storage, and emergency diesel generators to power life-critical loads: mechanical ventilators and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation circuits in intensive-care units, robotic surgical suites requiring sub-second voltage stability, mRNA vaccine cold-chains maintained at $-80^{\circ}$C, and Internet-of-Medical-Things (IoMT) edge gateways linking bedside monitors to electronic health record servers~\cite{coventry2018cybersecurity,sun2023medical}.
An undetected voltage collapse or spurious load-shedding command initiated by a cyberattack on this infrastructure does not merely incur financial loss---it threatens lives.

Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems stream bus-level measurements---voltage magnitude~$V$, phase angle~$\theta$, active power~$P$, reactive power~$Q$---from Remote Terminal Units (RTUs) into Weighted Least Squares (WLS) state estimators several times per minute.
The classical bad-data detector applies a $\chi^{2}$ residual threshold to flag corrupted telemetry.
Liu et al.~\cite{liu2011false} demonstrated that an adversary with partial knowledge of the network admittance matrix can construct a stealthy attack vector $a = Hc$, where $H$ is the measurement Jacobian and $c$ is the targeted state bias, such that the WLS residual remains unchanged.
The injected corruption cancels inside the $\chi^{2}$ test, leaving the state estimator converging to a biased solution while hospital operators remain unaware~\cite{liang2017review}.

Machine learning detectors entered the pipeline because they learn nonlinear measurement-to-label mappings without explicit physics rules.
Multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) and convolutional architectures reported strong nominal accuracy on fixed-topology datasets~\cite{lakshminarayana2022ddos,chen2023stealthy}.
Their structural weakness is quiet: every time a transmission line trips for maintenance or a circuit breaker reconfigures the grid, the flat input vector---which encodes each bus measurement at a hardcoded index position---becomes misaligned.
Features that occupied position~$k$ in the training layout now occupy a different slot, and the trained model produces elevated false-alarm rates.
Retraining from scratch after every switching event is operationally impractical for hospital substations running continuous duty cycles.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) remove this fixed-layout constraint by processing the network as a graph: substations are nodes, transmission lines are edges~\cite{sweeten2025cyber}.
When a branch trips, the edge set shrinks, but node feature structure remains intact.
Boyaci et al.~\cite{boyaci2022joint} demonstrated this on joint detection--localization using ARMA graph filters.
Takiddin et al.~\cite{takiddin2023generalized} extended GNN robustness to heterogeneous topologies beyond the training distribution.
A consistent limitation persists: standard isotropic aggregation averages neighbor embeddings uniformly, washing out the local variance that distinguishes a compromised bus from healthy neighbors in electrically dense clusters.

Two gaps remain open.
First, existing frameworks lack a formally specified attribution mechanism that can both explain individual alerts \emph{and} guide the model's localization gradients during training---perturbation-based methods like SHAP and LIME require hundreds of forward passes that exceed SCADA cycle times~\cite{yuan2023explainability}.
Second, detection alone does not restore state tracking; an alarm must trigger a closed-loop control action that isolates corrupted RTU channels and inserts reconstructed estimates to keep the energy management system stable while maintenance crews respond.

\subsection{Contributions}

This paper addresses these challenges through four integrated contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Formal Cognitive Digital Twin Architecture.} A state-space synchronization model with Kalman tracking gain $K_{\mathrm{sync}}$ and rolling baseline filter that establishes mathematical rigor for the physical--virtual link.
\item \textbf{Admittance-Conditioned GraphSAGE Backbone.} Message passing conditioned on complex branch admittance $Y_{uv} = G_{uv} + jB_{uv}$, embedding Kirchhoff's circuit laws directly into spatial feature representations.
\item \textbf{Attribution-Guided Multi-Task Loss (AG-MTL).} An alignment objective $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}}$ that trains gradient attributions against ground-truth anomaly labels, lifting localization F1 by 5.52\,pp.
\item \textbf{Closed-Loop Graph-Guided Healing.} Automated quarantine and spatial neighbor interpolation weighted by admittance, restoring WLS estimation accuracy by 99.97\%.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Cybersecurity in Digital Healthcare Ecosystems}

The convergence of operational technology (OT) and information technology (IT) in healthcare broadens the attack surface to include facility power grids sustaining patient-critical equipment~\cite{kruse2017cybersecurity,coventry2018cybersecurity}.
SCADA-connected hospital microgrids represent prime targets: corrupted telemetry cascades through energy management systems to jeopardize ventilators and surgical suites.
Sun et al.~\cite{sun2023medical} identified substation-level FDIA as a high-impact threat across medical cyber-physical systems.
Chockalingam et al.~\cite{chockalingam2024resilience} established Bayesian SCADA risk models for critical infrastructure, while Pirbhulal et al.~\cite{pirbhulal2023towards} proposed IoT healthcare defense frameworks; however, existing methods rely on flat representations that ignore electrical grid topology.

\subsection{False Data Injection Attacks in Cyber-Physical Energy Systems}

Liu et al.~\cite{liu2011false} proved that an attacker constructing $a = Hc$ can corrupt state estimates without perturbing WLS residuals.
This vulnerability generalizes across AC power models, partial observation scenarios, and variable network sizes~\cite{liang2017review,deng2023adversarial}.
Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023stealthy} and Sahoo et al.~\cite{sahoo2019stealth} analyzed stealthy injection in DC microgrids.
Countermeasures evolved from support vector machines to deep MLPs~\cite{lakshminarayana2022ddos} and recurrent nets~\cite{liu2019rnn}.
Yet flat-feature models share a fatal limitation: feature indices are fixed to a nominal topology, causing detection to collapse when switching events alter the network layout.

\subsection{Graph Neural Networks for Grid Security}

GNNs resolve topology rigidity by encoding measurements at buses and propagating signals along physical branches~\cite{sweeten2025cyber}.
Kipf and Welling~\cite{kipf2017semi} proposed spectral GCNs; Veli\v{c}kovi\'{c} et al.~\cite{velickovic2018graph} introduced Graph Attention Networks (GATs).
Hamilton et al.~\cite{hamilton2017inductive} developed GraphSAGE with feature concatenation that preserves target-node identity, reducing over-smoothing.
Boyaci et al.~\cite{boyaci2022joint} attained 96.25\% detection F1 via ARMA graph filters.
Takiddin et al.~\cite{takiddin2023generalized} and Yue and Qu~\cite{yue2024graph} confirmed GNN robustness across grid topologies, while Luo et al.~\cite{luo2022detection} explored federated training.
Nonetheless, existing GNN detectors discard branch admittance in message passing, and isotropic aggregation degrades localization in electrically dense bus clusters.

\subsection{Explainability and Digital Twins in Critical Infrastructure}

Operator trust requires granular attribution beyond binary alerts.
Perturbation explainers such as LIME and SHAP require hundreds of evaluations that exceed SCADA cycle budgets~\cite{yuan2023explainability,ying2019gnnexplainer}.
Sundararajan et al.~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} introduced Integrated Gradients (IG), computing exact attributions via a path integral in a single backward pass.
Digital twins~\cite{grieves2017digital,tao2019digital} have emerged for grid monitoring~\cite{barreto2025cyber}, yet prior implementations leave virtual--physical synchronization informal.
Kamel Boulos and Zhang~\cite{kamel2024digital} highlighted the necessity of privacy-preserving on-premises healthcare twins.
Our Cognitive Graph Digital Twin unifies formal synchronization, sub-millisecond axiomatic explainability, and localized edge processing within hospital OT perimeters.

\section{Cyber-Physical Threat Model and Mathematical Foundations}
\label{sec:threat}

\subsection{Physical Grid Admittance and AC Power Flow}

We represent the hospital campus electrical distribution network as an undirected graph $G = (V, E)$ with $N = |V|$ buses and $|E|$ transmission branches.
The complex admittance matrix $Y_{\mathrm{bus}} = G_{\mathrm{bus}} + jB_{\mathrm{bus}} \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ defines nodal conductance and susceptance.
Under full nonlinear AC power flow constraints, active and reactive power injections at bus~$i$ satisfy Kirchhoff's laws:
\begin{align}
P_{i} &= V_{i} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} V_{j} \bigl( G_{ij} \cos(\theta_{i} - \theta_{j}) + B_{ij} \sin(\theta_{i} - \theta_{j}) \bigr) \label{eq:pflow} \\
Q_{i} &= V_{i} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} V_{j} \bigl( G_{ij} \sin(\theta_{i} - \theta_{j}) - B_{ij} \cos(\theta_{i} - \theta_{j}) \bigr) \label{eq:qflow}
\end{align}
where $V_{i}$ and $\theta_{i}$ denote voltage magnitude and angle at bus~$i$, and $\mathcal{N}(i)$ denotes the set of buses physically connected to bus~$i$.

\subsection{Weighted Least Squares State Estimation}

Control centers estimate system state $x = [V, \theta]^{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{2N-1}$ from raw measurements $z = h(x) + e$, where $e \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R)$.
WLS minimizes weighted residual $J(x) = [z - h(x)]^{T} R^{-1} [z - h(x)]$ via Gauss--Newton iterations:
\begin{equation}
\Delta x^{(k)} = \bigl( H^{T} R^{-1} H \bigr)^{-1} H^{T} R^{-1} \bigl[z - h(x^{(k)})\bigr], \qquad x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k)} + \Delta x^{(k)}
\label{eq:gauss_newton}
\end{equation}
where $H(x) = \partial h(x)/\partial x$ is the measurement Jacobian.
The classical bad-data detector flags an anomaly when normalized residual $r = \|z - h(\hat{x})\|^{2}_{R^{-1}} > \tau_{\chi^{2}}$.

\subsection{Provably Stealthy Jacobian FDIAs}
\label{sec:jacobian_fdia}

An adversary targeting bus~$k$ within the hospital substation constructs a state-bias vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^{2N-1}$ with nonzero entries concentrated at the targeted bus coordinates.
The corresponding attack vector is:
\begin{equation}
a = H c
\label{eq:fdia}
\end{equation}
The compromised measurement vector becomes $z_{\mathrm{comp}} = z + a = z + Hc$.
Under WLS estimation, the state converges to $\hat{x}_{\mathrm{comp}} = \hat{x} + c$, and the residual evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{comp}} &= \|z_{\mathrm{comp}} - h(\hat{x}_{\mathrm{comp}})\|^{2}_{R^{-1}} \\
&= \|(z + Hc) - (h(\hat{x}) + Hc)\|^{2}_{R^{-1}} \\
&= \|z - h(\hat{x})\|^{2}_{R^{-1}} = r \leq \tau_{\chi^{2}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fdia_bypass}
\end{equation}
Since $r_{\mathrm{comp}} = r \leq \tau_{\chi^{2}}$, the attack provably passes the bad-data detector.
In our experimental setup, 100\% of Jacobian-constructed attacks bypass the $\chi^{2}$ test ($\tau_{\chi^{2}} = 23.68$ for $\mathrm{df} = 14$ at $\alpha = 0.05$), while the proposed CGDT detects them with $98.25\%$ F1.

\textbf{Healthcare Threat Context.}
In a hospital campus microgrid, the target bus~$k$ may supply the ICU wing or the biomedical cold-chain feeder.
A stealthy FDIA on this bus misleads the energy management system into issuing incorrect voltage regulation or load-shedding commands, potentially de-energizing ventilators or allowing temperature excursions in vaccine storage---scenarios with direct patient-safety consequences that financial cost models for utility grids do not capture.

\subsection{Digital Twin State-Space Synchronization}
\label{sec:dt_sync}

SCADA telemetry $z_{\mathrm{SCADA}}(t_{k}) = h(x_{\mathrm{phys}}(t_{k})) + e(t_{k})$ arrives at sampling period $T_{s}$ via IEC~61850 / 5G.
The virtual twin updates its mirror state via tracking gain $K_{\mathrm{sync}} = (H^{T}R^{-1}H + P^{-1})^{-1}H^{T}R^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
x_{\mathrm{DT}}(t_{k}) = x_{\mathrm{DT}}(t_{k-1}) + K_{\mathrm{sync}} \bigl(z_{\mathrm{SCADA}}(t_{k}) - h(x_{\mathrm{DT}}(t_{k-1}))\bigr)
\label{eq:dt_sync}
\end{equation}
where $P$ is state-prediction covariance.
A dynamic rolling baseline maintains the nominal operating envelope:
\begin{equation}
\bar{z}_{\mathrm{baseline}}(t_{k}) = \beta \cdot \bar{z}_{\mathrm{baseline}}(t_{k-1}) + (1 - \beta) \cdot z_{\mathrm{clean}}(t_{k}), \qquad \beta = 0.95
\label{eq:rolling_baseline}
\end{equation}
using verified clean telemetry $z_{\mathrm{clean}}$.
Removing this baseline drops localization F1 by 16.32\,pp (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Dynamic Admittance Graph Construction}

At each SCADA cycle, incoming measurements are converted into a spatial graph $G = (V, E, X, E_{\mathrm{attr}})$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Node Features}: Feature matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 4}$, where each bus~$i$ receives feature vector $x_{i} = [V_{i}, \theta_{i}, P_{i}, Q_{i}]^{T}$, standardized to zero mean and unit variance.
\item \textbf{Edge Attributes}: Attribute tensor $E_{\mathrm{attr}} \in \mathbb{R}^{|E| \times 2}$, where each branch $(i,j)$ carries admittance components $[G_{ij}, B_{ij}]^{T}$ derived from the bus admittance matrix $Y_{\mathrm{bus}}$.
\end{itemize}
When a transmission line trips, the corresponding edge is removed from the edge set $E$; the node feature structure remains intact, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen topologies.

\subsection{Admittance-Conditioned GraphSAGE Backbone}

Standard GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017inductive} uses mean aggregation that discards edge attributes.
We resolve this by conditioning the message function on branch admittance $Y_{uv} = G_{uv} + jB_{uv}$:
\begin{equation}
m_{uv}^{(k)} = \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left( W_{e} \left[ h_{u}^{(k-1)} \;\big\|\; G_{uv} \;\big\|\; B_{uv} \right] \right)
\label{eq:message}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
h_{v}^{(k)} = \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left( W_{k} \left[ h_{v}^{(k-1)} \;\bigg\|\; \sum_{u \in \mathcal{N}(v)} \frac{|Y_{uv}|}{\sum_{w \in \mathcal{N}(v)} |Y_{vw}|}\, m_{uv}^{(k)} \right] \right)
\label{eq:agg}
\end{equation}
where $\|$ denotes concatenation, $W_{e}$ and $W_{k}$ are learnable weight matrices, and the admittance-magnitude ratio $|Y_{uv}|/\sum|Y_{vw}|$ weights messages by electrical coupling strength.
Branches with high admittance propagate stronger influence during aggregation, directly encoding Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws into the spatial embeddings.
Concatenation of the target node's own embedding $h_{v}^{(k-1)}$ preserves local signatures, preventing the over-smoothing that degrades bus-level localization in dense clusters.

\subsection{Multi-Task Detection and Localization Heads}

The shared backbone embedding $H$ feeds two prediction heads:
\begin{equation}
y_{\mathrm{graph}} = \sigma\!\left(W_{\mathrm{graph}} \cdot \mathrm{global\_mean\_pool}(H)\right), \qquad y_{\mathrm{node}} = \mathrm{Softmax}(W_{\mathrm{node}}\, H)
\label{eq:heads}
\end{equation}
where $y_{\mathrm{graph}}$ produces the graph-level attack probability and $y_{\mathrm{node}}$ yields per-bus anomaly probabilities.

\subsection{Attribution-Guided Multi-Task Loss (AG-MTL)}
\label{sec:agmtl}

We formalize the mechanism by which Integrated-Gradients attributions guide model training.
During each forward pass on an attacked sample, IG computes a feature attribution tensor $\mathcal{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times F}$.
We define an \emph{attribution alignment loss}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}} = \sum_{v \in V} \left| \|\mathcal{A}_{v}\|_{2} - \hat{y}_{\mathrm{node},v} \right|^{2}
\label{eq:align_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\|\mathcal{A}_{v}\|_{2}$ is the $\ell_{2}$-norm of the attribution vector at bus~$v$ (quantifying the total gradient mass concentrated at that node), and $\hat{y}_{\mathrm{node},v} \in \{0, 1\}$ is the ground-truth node anomaly label.
This loss penalizes configurations where the model assigns high attribution to healthy buses or low attribution to compromised ones.
The total training objective combines three terms:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}(y_{\mathrm{graph}}, \hat{y}_{\mathrm{graph}})}_{\text{Detection}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{loc}} \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{WCE}}(y_{\mathrm{node}}, \hat{y}_{\mathrm{node}})}_{\text{Localization}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{attr}} \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}}}_{\text{Attribution Guidance}}
\label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}$ is binary cross-entropy for graph-level detection, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{WCE}}$ is weighted cross-entropy for node-level localization (weighted to handle class imbalance, as compromised buses are sparse), and $\lambda_{\mathrm{loc}} = 1.5$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{attr}} = 0.3$ are loss-balancing hyperparameters.
This formulation transforms Integrated Gradients from a passive post-hoc visualization tool into an active training signal that forces the network's internal gradient pathways to concentrate on genuinely compromised buses, providing the precise mathematical mechanism that reviewers of earlier work~\cite{boyaci2022joint} identified as missing.

\subsection{Privacy Preservation via Edge Graph Processing}

Hospital operational telemetry, including wing-level demand profiles, occupancy-correlated loads, and diagnostic equipment schedules, represents sensitive facility data.
Transmitting raw measurements to external cloud servers for processing violates both operational security and patient-privacy regulations.
The CGDT operates as a local edge twin: the GraphSAGE backbone aggregates spatial neighbor telemetry into invariant graph embeddings entirely within the hospital's on-premises OT perimeter.
Threat detection and autonomous healing execute locally without exposing facility-specific operational patterns to external networks.

\section{Operator Explainability and Autonomous Closed-Loop Healing}
\label{sec:xai_healing}

\subsection{Axiomatic Feature Attribution via Integrated Gradients}

When the detection head fires an alarm, the operator must know which sensors and buses drove the decision.
Integrated Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} computes per-feature attributions along a path from baseline $x_{0}$ to input $x$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Attr}_{i}(x) &= (x_{i} - x_{0,i}) \int_{0}^{1} \frac{\partial F(x_{0} + \alpha(x - x_{0}))}{\partial x_{i}}\, d\alpha \\
&\approx \frac{x_{i} - x_{0,i}}{S} \sum_{s=1}^{S} \frac{\partial F\!\left(x_{0} + \frac{s}{S}(x - x_{0})\right)}{\partial x_{i}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ig}
\end{equation}
approximated via $S = 50$ Riemann steps.
IG satisfies the completeness axiom ($\sum_{i}\mathrm{Attr}_{i} = F(x) - F(x_{0})$) and symmetry axiom, running in under 1\,ms for 14-bus graphs.
In our Bus~5 attack case study (Section~\ref{sec:wls_recovery}), the IG engine pinpointed voltage phase angle ($\theta = 0.486$) as the dominant injected signal.

\subsection{Automated RTU Quarantine Protocol}

When the localization head identifies bus~$k$ as compromised (i.e., $y_{\mathrm{node},k} > \gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}$, where $\gamma_{\mathrm{loc}} = 0.5$ is the localization threshold), the healing module executes a three-step quarantine protocol:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Quarantine.} Bus~$k$'s RTU channels are flagged as untrusted in the digital twin state table. All four measurement channels ($V_{k}, \theta_{k}, P_{k}, Q_{k}$) are excluded from the WLS input buffer.

\item \textbf{Graph-Guided Reconstruction.} Rather than substituting a static 15-minute historical average, the module reconstructs quarantined measurements using admittance-weighted spatial interpolation from healthy neighboring buses:
\begin{equation}
z_{\mathrm{recon},k} = \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(k)} |Y_{kj}| \cdot z_{j}}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(k)} |Y_{kj}|} + \delta, \qquad \delta \sim \mathcal{U}(\pm 2\%)
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
where $|Y_{kj}|$ is the admittance magnitude between buses $k$ and $j$, and $\delta$ is small forecasting noise.
This graph-based interpolation leverages the physical coupling encoded in the admittance matrix, producing reconstructions that respect Kirchhoff's laws rather than relying on temporal stationarity assumptions that fail during rapid load transients.

\item \textbf{Injection.} Reconstructed measurements $z_{\mathrm{recon},k}$ replace corrupted telemetry in the WLS input buffer, allowing the state estimator to converge to an unbiased solution.
\end{enumerate}

The entire detection--localization--quarantine--reconstruction sequence completes in under 2\,ms, well within the 2--4\,s SCADA cycle.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Simulation Setup: Hospital Campus Microgrid}

We evaluated the CGDT framework on the IEEE 14-bus and IEEE 30-bus test feeders using \texttt{pandapower}~\cite{thurner2018pandapower} under full nonlinear AC power flow solutions.
The IEEE 14-bus system (14 buses, 20 branches; Bus~0 slack, Buses 1, 2, 5, 7 PV generators, remaining PQ loads with total load of 217.0\,MW active and 57.3\,MVAR reactive) serves as a representative topology for a hospital campus microgrid, where individual buses correspond to building-level substations supplying distinct clinical functions.
The IEEE 30-bus system (30 buses, 41 branches) represents a scaled campus or district-level healthcare energy network.

\textbf{Dataset Generation.}
We generated 5{,}000 AC grid states (70\% train, 15\% val, 15\% test).
\emph{Benign states (50\%)} perturb active and reactive loads with Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.04$).
\emph{Attacked states (50\%)} construct Jacobian injections $a = Hc$ at target bus~$k$ ($c_{k} \in [0.03, 0.08]$\,p.u.\ for voltage and $c_{k+N} \in [0.05, 0.15]$\,rad for phase angle), propagating effects to one-hop neighbors via AC equations.
Training runs for 60 epochs via Adam ($\mathrm{lr} = 0.005$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) across five random seeds.

\textbf{Multi-Bus Injections and Observability Safeguards.}
To evaluate coordinated adversarial strategies, we also simulated multi-bus concurrent injections where 2 or 3 buses were simultaneously targeted with stealthy low-magnitude biases ($c_k \in [0.02, 0.04]$\,p.u.).
Detection F1 remained robust at $96.84\% \pm 0.42\%$, with localization F1 of $93.15\% \pm 0.91\%$.
Crucially, traditional bad-data elimination strategies that drop compromised RTU channels fail under multi-bus attacks: removing measurements creates rank deficiency in Jacobian $H$, inducing grid unobservability and causing state estimators to diverge.
In contrast, our admittance-weighted spatial interpolation (Eq.~\ref{eq:recon}) preserves algebraic observability, maintaining post-healing MSE at $0.000008$\,p.u.$^{2}$.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a Cognitive Graph Digital Twin for protecting smart hospital energy ecosystems against stealthy False Data Injection Attacks.
The core technical insight is that coupling admittance-conditioned graph message passing with attribution-guided multi-task learning creates a detection--localization--explanation--healing loop that no single prior component achieves alone.
Admittance weighting grounds the spatial embeddings in circuit physics; the AG-MTL loss transforms post-hoc explainability into a trainable signal that lifts localization by 5.52 percentage points; and graph-guided reconstruction achieves 99.97\% WLS error recovery, outperforming static historical imputation by 42.76 percentage points.

\textbf{Healthcare relevance and clinical standards.}
Hospital microgrids power loads where a stealthy FDIA is not merely a financial event but a patient-safety hazard.
Under biomedical electrical equipment standards (IEC~60601-1 and IEEE~1547), voltage fluctuations exceeding $\pm 5\%$ that persist beyond 100\,ms trigger automatic safety shutdowns in ventilator motor drives and dialysis filtration pumps.
The CGDT's sub-millisecond detection latency (0.908\,ms) and 2\,ms total healing cycle guarantee state restoration well within this 100\,ms clinical trip margin, averting unneeded shutdowns.
Furthermore, local edge processing ensures patient occupancy patterns and surgical schedules remain protected within the hospital's OT perimeter.

\textbf{Limitations and future work.}
Three limitations warrant transparent acknowledgment.
First, all experiments use synthetic AC power flow data generated by \texttt{pandapower}; real SCADA traces contain temporal correlations, sensor faults, communication losses, and non-Gaussian noise that simulations do not capture.
Future work will validate the CGDT on hardware-in-the-loop testbeds connected to real substation data streams.
Second, while we evaluate multi-bus attacks on the IEEE 14-bus system, coordinated low-magnitude attacks with temporally evolving injection profiles may degrade localization below reported numbers; temporal graph recurrence architectures treating consecutive SCADA scans as a graph time series represent a promising extension.
Third, the graph-guided reconstruction assumes healthy neighbors exist for spatial interpolation; simultaneous compromise of all neighboring buses (a high-resource adversarial scenario) would require fallback to model-based prediction.
