% ABSTRACT
% ===================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Smart hospital campuses rely on localized microgrids to sustain intensive-care ventilators, surgical robotics, and biomedical cold chains.
A stealthy False Data Injection Attack (FDIA) on substation Remote Terminal Units can corrupt the energy-management state estimator while evading its $\chi^{2}$ residual check, placing patient-critical loads at risk.
We introduce a Cognitive Graph Digital Twin (CGDT) that mirrors the hospital electrical topology as a dynamic admittance graph and runs a multi-task Admittance-Conditioned GraphSAGE backbone for simultaneous attack detection and bus-level localization in a single forward pass at 0.908\,ms latency.
To bridge the gap between post-hoc saliency diagnostics and inductive training, our Attribution-Guided Multi-Task Loss forces internal backpropagation gradients to align directly with true substation anomaly locations, driving a 5.52 percentage-point improvement in localization F1.
Dynamic physical-to-virtual coherence is governed by a state-space tracking filter coupled with an adaptive exponential baseline.
Upon detecting an attack, a closed-loop healing module quarantines compromised sensor channels and reconstructs measurements via graph-guided spatial interpolation, reducing Weighted Least Squares estimation error by 99.97\%.
On the IEEE 14-bus test feeder under full nonlinear AC power flow, the CGDT achieves $98.25\%\pm0.34\%$ detection F1 and $97.62\%\pm0.84\%$ localization F1 across five random seeds, and retains $92.80\%$ detection F1 under zero-shot topology reconfigurations without retraining.

\keywords{Graph Digital Twin \and Healthcare Cybersecurity \and GraphSAGE \and Explainable AI \and False Data Injection Attacks \and Smart Hospital Microgrid}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In tertiary healthcare complexes, electrical power is inextricably linked to patient survival.
Modern hospital campuses rely on localized microgrids that combine utility interconnections, rooftop photovoltaics, battery storage banks, and diesel generators to energize critical medical loads~\cite{coventry2018cybersecurity,sun2023medical}.
These loads cannot tolerate instability: surgical robotics demand millisecond voltage consistency, intensive-care ventilators require uninterrupted feeds, and vaccine cold-chains depend on constant refrigeration at $-80^{\circ}$C.
Any uncoordinated breaker trip, false load shedding, or voltage sag directly endangers human life.

Substation Remote Terminal Units (RTUs) stream voltage magnitudes, phase angles, and power flows ($V, \theta, P, Q$) into Weighted Least Squares (WLS) state estimators over IEC~61850 substation buses.
Control centers routinely rely on normalized $\chi^{2}$ residual tests to weed out random transducer noise and faulty metering.
Yet this classical check fails entirely against coordinated vectors.
By exploiting the topology-dependent measurement Jacobian $H$, an adversary synthesizes an injection $a = Hc$ that shifts state estimates by an arbitrary vector $c$ while leaving algebraic measurement residuals entirely intact~\cite{liu2011false,liang2017review}.
The corrupted telemetry slips past the detector unflagged; automated dispatch software then acts on compromised grid profiles.

Prior security schemes leaned heavily on tabular classifiers---ranging from support vector machines to multi-layer perceptrons and fixed-kernel convolutional nets~\cite{lakshminarayana2022ddos,chen2023stealthy}.
These tabular baselines break whenever the grid flexes.
Hospital distribution feeders undergo frequent branch switching, tie-line transfers, and transformer tap adjustments.
Each physical breaker toggle changes sensor ordering, blinding fixed-input neural networks or triggering false tripping alarms.
Retraining these models on the fly during 2--4\,s SCADA sampling windows remains out of the question.

Graph neural networks (GNNs) solve this layout rigidity by treating substations as nodes and physical lines as edges~\cite{sweeten2025cyber}.
Tripping a breaker simply toggles an entry in the graph adjacency matrix without disturbing node vector dimensions.
While spatial wavelets~\cite{boyaci2022joint} and isotropic message passing~\cite{takiddin2023generalized} accommodate switching events, conventional GNNs treat all incident branches identically.
They ignore complex branch admittances ($Y_{uv} = G_{uv} + jB_{uv}$).
By averaging nodal representations isotropically across neighbors, standard convolutions wash out high-frequency voltage and angle discrepancies across adjacent electrical corridors.

Clinical facilities tolerate neither unverified black-box alerts nor sluggish incident response.
Perturbation-based explainers such as SHAP or LIME require hundreds of Monte Carlo passes, exceeding real-time SCADA deadlines by orders of magnitude~\cite{yuan2023explainability}.
Moreover, issuing passive alarms without mitigating the bad data leaves life-support circuits exposed to automated shedding.
To safeguard critical healthcare microgrids, an anomaly framework must pin down compromised buses within sub-second intervals and synthesize clean replacement telemetry before downstream protection relays trip.

\subsection{Key Contributions}

We address these operational gaps by formulating an explainable Cognitive Graph Digital Twin (CGDT) tailored to hospital electrical infrastructure.
Specific technical contributions include:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Physics-Informed GraphSAGE Formulation}: Rather than relying on unweighted adjacency matrices, we condition neighborhood message passing and spatial pooling on complex branch admittances ($Y_{uv} = G_{uv} + jB_{uv}$). This preserves sharp electrical gradients across low-impedance paths and prevents isotropic low-pass feature blurring.
\item \textbf{Continuous Twin Synchronization}: By pairing steady-state Kalman gain tracking with an exponential baseline filter ($\beta = 0.95$), the twin tracks normal diurnal load swings while isolating stealthy sub-threshold biases that otherwise degrade bus localization by 16.32\,pp.
\item \textbf{Attribution-Guided Multi-Task Training (AG-MTL)}: We integrate an attribution alignment loss into training using single-pass Integrated Gradients. Penalizing gradient leakage into benign feeders forces the network to concentrate spatial features on true injection sites, boosting localization F1 by 5.52\,pp.
\item \textbf{Sub-2\,ms Autonomous State Reconstruction}: When malicious streams are quarantined, an admittance-weighted spatial estimator interpolates missing bus values from uncorrupted neighbors, preserving measurement Jacobian rank and restoring WLS estimation accuracy by 99.97\%.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Cybersecurity in Digital Healthcare Ecosystems}

Bridging clinical hospital IT infrastructure with operational substation control dramatically widens institutional vulnerability~\cite{kruse2017cybersecurity,coventry2018cybersecurity}.
Unlike commercial facilities, hospital energy microgrids power intensive-care wards and surgical suites where unannounced outages or severe voltage dips present immediate patient mortality risks.
Malicious telemetry manipulation within healthcare SCADA channels represents an especially destructive vector~\cite{sun2023medical}.
While recent assessments have modeled healthcare risks through Bayesian graphs~\cite{chockalingam2024resilience} or perimeter IoT monitors~\cite{pirbhulal2023towards}, such frameworks process sensor values as decoupled scalar streams.
They neglect branch impedances, line admittances, and Kirchhoff's laws, leaving them blind to coordinated injection vectors.

\subsection{False Data Injection in Cyber-Physical Energy Systems}

Classical state estimation relies on the measurement residual to spot bad data.
However, Liu et al.~\cite{liu2011false} demonstrated that an adversary possessing knowledge of the measurement Jacobian $H$ can craft an unobservable state bias $a = Hc$ that cancels perfectly inside the residual calculation.
This stealth condition generalizes readily to full AC power flow formulations, distributed microgrids, and partially observed topologies~\cite{liang2017review,deng2023adversarial}, as well as DC microgrid links~\cite{chen2023stealthy,sahoo2019stealth}.
Early defenses attempted detection via tabular classifiers, including support vector machines, deep neural networks~\cite{lakshminarayana2022ddos}, and recurrent units~\cite{liu2019rnn}.
These rigid baselines, however, hard-code input dimensions to specific sensor channels.
Routine distribution switching---such as isolating a feeder or altering a transformer tap---scrambles channel order and induces persistent false positives.

\subsection{Graph Representation Learning for Power System Security}

Treating electric networks as attributed graphs provides natural invariance to switching operations~\cite{sweeten2025cyber}.
Early models applied spectral convolutions~\cite{kipf2017semi} or spatial attention weights~\cite{velickovic2018graph}.
To scale spatial aggregations across changing topologies, GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017inductive} samples local neighborhoods inductively.
In power networks, Boyaci et al.~\cite{boyaci2022joint} employed ARMA wavelets, while Takiddin et al.~\cite{takiddin2023generalized} and Yue and Qu~\cite{yue2024graph} confirmed topological flexibility across line configurations.
Luo et al.~\cite{luo2022detection} further introduced federated setups to protect operational privacy.
Nonetheless, these architectures routinely rely on unweighted or binary adjacency matrices.
Failing to weight message propagation by complex branch admittances ($Y_{uv} = G_{uv} + jB_{uv}$) creates an isotropic low-pass filtering effect, obscuring localized voltage anomalies across low-impedance branches.

\subsection{Explainability and Digital Twins in Critical Infrastructure}

Control room operators cannot act on unverified black-box alerts.
Yet sampling-based attribution methods (e.g., LIME, SHAP, or GNNExplainer) require hundreds of model evaluations per inference cycle, rendering them impractical for online SCADA operations~\cite{yuan2023explainability,ying2019gnnexplainer}.
Integrated Gradients (IG)~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} overcomes this hurdle by evaluating path integrals along straight-line trajectories in a single backward pass, satisfying the completeness axiom without stochastic perturbation passes.
Simultaneously, digital twin principles~\cite{grieves2017digital,tao2019digital} have gained traction for power infrastructure monitoring~\cite{barreto2025cyber} and healthcare facilities~\cite{kamel2024digital}.
Building upon these concepts, we unite continuous state-space tracking with admittance-guided graph convolutions to deliver real-time intrusion localization and auditable feature explanations within milliseconds.

\section{Cyber-Physical Threat Model and Mathematical Foundations}
\label{sec:threat}

\subsection{Physical Grid Admittance and AC Power Flow}

We represent the hospital campus electrical distribution network as an undirected graph $G = (V, E)$ with $N = |V|$ buses and $|E|$ transmission branches.
The complex admittance matrix $Y_{\mathrm{bus}} = G_{\mathrm{bus}} + jB_{\mathrm{bus}} \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ defines nodal conductance and susceptance.
Under full nonlinear AC power flow constraints, active and reactive power injections at bus~$i$ satisfy Kirchhoff's laws:
\begin{align}
P_{i} &= V_{i} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} V_{j} \bigl( G_{ij} \cos(\theta_{i} - \theta_{j}) + B_{ij} \sin(\theta_{i} - \theta_{j}) \bigr) \label{eq:pflow} \\
Q_{i} &= V_{i} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} V_{j} \bigl( G_{ij} \sin(\theta_{i} - \theta_{j}) - B_{ij} \cos(\theta_{i} - \theta_{j}) \bigr) \label{eq:qflow}
\end{align}
where $V_{i}$ and $\theta_{i}$ denote voltage magnitude and angle at bus~$i$, and $\mathcal{N}(i)$ denotes the set of buses physically connected to bus~$i$.

\subsection{Weighted Least Squares State Estimation}

Power system state estimation determines the state vector $x = [V, \theta]^{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{2N-1}$ from measurement telemetry $z = h(x) + e$, where $e \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R)$ represents sensor noise with covariance $R$.
The WLS estimator minimizes the weighted residual $J(x) = [z - h(x)]^{T} R^{-1} [z - h(x)]$ using Gauss--Newton iterations:
\begin{equation}
\Delta x^{(k)} = \bigl( H^{T} R^{-1} H \bigr)^{-1} H^{T} R^{-1} \bigl[z - h(x^{(k)})\bigr], \qquad x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k)} + \Delta x^{(k)}
\label{eq:gauss_newton}
\end{equation}
where $H(x) = \partial h(x)/\partial x$ is the measurement Jacobian.
Conventional bad-data detection flags potential measurement anomalies when the normalized residual energy $r = \|z - h(\hat{x})\|^{2}_{R^{-1}}$ exceeds a threshold $\tau_{\chi^{2}}$, determined from a chi-square distribution with degrees of freedom equal to the number of measurements minus system states.

\subsection{Stealthy False Data Injection Attacks}
\label{sec:jacobian_fdia}

An attacker targeting substation bus~$k$ seeks to induce a specific state error $c \in \mathbb{R}^{2N-1}$ while evading the $\chi^{2}$ residual detector.
Using knowledge of the network admittance structure, the attacker constructs the injection vector according to:
\begin{equation}
a = H c
\label{eq:fdia}
\end{equation}
When injected into the communication stream, the received telemetry becomes $z_{\mathrm{comp}} = z + a = z + Hc$.
The resulting state estimate shifts to $\hat{x}_{\mathrm{comp}} = \hat{x} + c$.
Evaluating the post-attack residual yields:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{comp}} &= \|z_{\mathrm{comp}} - h(\hat{x}_{\mathrm{comp}})\|^{2}_{R^{-1}} \\
&= \|(z + Hc) - (h(\hat{x}) + Hc)\|^{2}_{R^{-1}} \\
&= \|z - h(\hat{x})\|^{2}_{R^{-1}} = r \leq \tau_{\chi^{2}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fdia_bypass}
\end{equation}
Because the injected shift cancels algebraically within the normalized quadratic norm ($r_{\mathrm{comp}} = r$), the WLS detector remains blind ($\tau_{\chi^{2}} = 23.68$ for $\mathrm{df} = 14$ at significance $\alpha = 0.05$).
Corrupted measurements pass directly into downstream dispatch algorithms without generating an alarm.

\textbf{Clinical Hazards in Hospital Distribution Networks.}
While commercial utilities primarily face economic redispatch losses during an attack, hospital distribution networks face direct clinical emergencies.
Falsifying state estimates at critical feeders---such as Bus~5, which feeds surgical operating rooms and intensive care ventilation---tricks automatic voltage regulators into improper tap changes or unwarranted branch isolation.
Standards like IEC~60601-1 and IEEE~1547 demand strict voltage stability; even transient sags can force sensitive life-support machinery onto backup emergency batteries or trigger autonomous equipment trips.
An undetected mathematical deception at the substation level thus translates into an immediate patient safety risk.

\label{sec:architecture}

The CGDT framework couples the hospital's cyber-physical distribution infrastructure to a virtual graph model and an automated self-healing pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:conceptual_framework}).

\subsection{Digital Twin State-Space Synchronization}
\label{sec:dt_sync}

Field telemetry $z_{\mathrm{SCADA}}(t_{k}) = h(x_{\mathrm{phys}}(t_{k})) + e(t_{k})$ streams from substation RTUs at polling cadence $T_{s}$ across IEC~61850 station buses.
To maintain physical-to-virtual alignment without amplifying transducer noise, the digital twin updates its mirrored state $x_{\mathrm{DT}}$ via steady-state tracking gain $K_{\mathrm{sync}} = (H^{T}R^{-1}H + P^{-1})^{-1}H^{T}R^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
x_{\mathrm{DT}}(t_{k}) = x_{\mathrm{DT}}(t_{k-1}) + K_{\mathrm{sync}} \bigl(z_{\mathrm{SCADA}}(t_{k}) - h(x_{\mathrm{DT}}(t_{k-1}))\bigr)
\label{eq:dt_sync}
\end{equation}
where $P$ denotes state-prediction covariance.
Concurrently, an exponential filter establishes an evolving operational baseline:
\begin{equation}
\bar{z}_{\mathrm{baseline}}(t_{k}) = \beta \cdot \bar{z}_{\mathrm{baseline}}(t_{k-1}) + (1 - \beta) \cdot z_{\mathrm{clean}}(t_{k}), \qquad \beta = 0.95
\label{eq:rolling_baseline}
\end{equation}
updated strictly using verified clean measurements $z_{\mathrm{clean}}$.
Without this adaptive baseline, slow diurnal load ramps mask small adversarial injection biases, precipitating a 16.32\,pp collapse in localization F1 (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Dynamic Admittance Graph Construction}

At each sampling step, physical telemetry is mapped to an attributed graph $G = (V, E, X, E_{\mathrm{attr}})$.
The vertex feature matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 4}$ gathers four-dimensional state vectors $x_{i} = [V_{i}, \theta_{i}, P_{i}, Q_{i}]^{T}$ for each substation bus $i \in V$, normalized over historical operational envelopes.
Physical branch connectivity is stored in edge tensor $E_{\mathrm{attr}} \in \mathbb{R}^{|E| \times 2}$, where each entry pairs the line conductance $G_{ij}$ and susceptance $B_{ij}$ from network admittance matrix $Y_{\mathrm{bus}}$.
When protective relays trip or maintenance crews open a breaker, the corresponding tuple is excised from edge set $E$.
Excising open branches alters adjacency entries while leaving nodal feature dimensions untouched; hence, the graph backbone absorbs topological switching on the fly without retraining.

\subsection{Admittance-Conditioned GraphSAGE Backbone}

Standard GraphSAGE models~\cite{hamilton2017inductive} aggregate neighborhood features uniformly across links, ignoring branch impedances.
In physical power distribution networks, however, electrical coupling is dictated by complex branch admittances $Y_{uv} = G_{uv} + jB_{uv}$.
To embed these circuit constraints directly into latent feature representations, we condition both message generation and spatial aggregation on branch admittance:
\begin{equation}
m_{uv}^{(k)} = \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left( W_{e} \left[ h_{u}^{(k-1)} \;\big\|\; G_{uv} \;\big\|\; B_{uv} \right] \right)
\label{eq:message}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
h_{v}^{(k)} = \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left( W_{k} \left[ h_{v}^{(k-1)} \;\bigg\|\; \sum_{u \in \mathcal{N}(v)} \frac{|Y_{uv}|}{\sum_{w \in \mathcal{N}(v)} |Y_{vw}|}\, m_{uv}^{(k)} \right] \right)
\label{eq:agg}
\end{equation}
where $\|$ denotes vector concatenation, and $W_{e}$, $W_{k}$ are trainable projection matrices.
Weighting inbound messages by normalized admittance $|Y_{uv}| / \sum_{w} |Y_{vw}|$ forces the spatial convolution to reflect Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws, granting stronger influence to electrically proximate substations.
Retaining the self-node state $h_{v}^{(k-1)}$ via residual skip concatenation prevents sharp localized injection anomalies from washing out through repeated neighbor smoothing.

\subsection{Multi-Task Output Heads}

From the final latent node matrix $H \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$, two parallel projection heads generate global and bus-level predictions:
\begin{equation}
y_{\mathrm{graph}} = \sigma\!\left(W_{\mathrm{graph}} \cdot \mathrm{global\_mean\_pool}(H)\right), \qquad y_{\mathrm{node}} = \mathrm{Softmax}(W_{\mathrm{node}}\, H)
\label{eq:heads}
\end{equation}
Here $y_{\mathrm{graph}} \in [0, 1]$ flags whether the microgrid is under attack, while $y_{\mathrm{node}} \in [0, 1]^{N}$ isolates the compromised substation buses.

\subsection{Attribution-Guided Multi-Task Loss (AG-MTL)}
\label{sec:agmtl}

Multi-hop message passing often causes gradient dispersion: backpropagating through dense admittance corridors inadvertently assigns feature sensitivity to uncorrupted neighbors.
To prevent this spatial blurring, we incorporate feature attribution directly into the training objective.
During each backward pass on attacked states, Integrated Gradients extracts the attribution tensor $\mathcal{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 4}$ across all vertices.
An attribution alignment loss penalizes discrepancy between bus attribution energy and true anomaly labels:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}} = \sum_{v \in V} \left| \|\mathcal{A}_{v}\|_{2} - \hat{y}_{\mathrm{node},v} \right|^{2}
\label{eq:align_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\|\mathcal{A}_{v}\|_{2}$ measures total gradient sensitivity at bus~$v$, and $\hat{y}_{\mathrm{node},v} \in \{0, 1\}$ denotes the binary ground-truth label.
If the model relies on spurious correlations at unattacked buses, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}}$ immediately penalizes the responsible weights.
The combined multi-task loss is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}(y_{\mathrm{graph}}, \hat{y}_{\mathrm{graph}})}_{\text{Detection}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{loc}} \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{WCE}}(y_{\mathrm{node}}, \hat{y}_{\mathrm{node}})}_{\text{Localization}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{attr}} \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}}}_{\text{Attribution Guidance}}
\label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}$ handles grid-level attack detection, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{WCE}}$ uses class-weighted cross-entropy to overcome the heavy class imbalance of sparse compromised buses, and weighting coefficients $\lambda_{\mathrm{loc}} = 1.5$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{attr}} = 0.3$ balance gradient magnitudes across tasks.

\subsection{On-Premises Edge Deployment}

Hospital electrical demand closely tracks clinical operations---including operating theatre schedules, diagnostic imaging loads, and patient occupancy.
Transmitting raw microgrid telemetry over external cloud interfaces poses both privacy and cybersecurity concerns under health data regulations.
The CGDT is therefore architected for bare-metal execution on substation edge servers within the isolated operational technology (OT) network.
By executing telemetry ingestion, neural inference, and healing algorithms entirely on site, the design ensures deterministic sub-millisecond execution without external network exposure.

\section{Explainability and Autonomous State Reconstruction}
\label{sec:xai_healing}

\subsection{Feature Attribution via Integrated Gradients}

Substation engineers must understand which physical sensor channels triggered an alarm before initiating breaker actions.
We resolve this via path-based Integrated Gradients (IG)~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic}.
Integrating gradients along a linear path between a nominal state baseline $x_0$ and the evaluated measurement vector $x$ attributes decision energy across input channels:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Attr}_{i}(x) &= (x_{i} - x_{0,i}) \int_{0}^{1} \frac{\partial F(x_{0} + \alpha(x - x_{0}))}{\partial x_{i}}\, d\alpha \\
&\approx \frac{x_{i} - x_{0,i}}{S} \sum_{s=1}^{S} \frac{\partial F\!\left(x_{0} + \frac{s}{S}(x - x_{0})\right)}{\partial x_{i}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ig}
\end{equation}
approximated using $S = 50$ Gauss--Legendre quadrature steps.
Crucially, IG satisfies the completeness axiom ($\sum_{i}\mathrm{Attr}_{i} = F(x) - F(x_{0})$) and implementation invariance.
Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:ig} across the 14-bus network requires just 0.81\,ms.
In a targeted test at Bus~5 (Section~\ref{sec:wls_recovery}), the path integral promptly identified phase angle distortion ($\theta = 0.486$) as the principal driver of the alarm.

\subsection{Sensor Quarantine and Admittance-Weighted Reconstruction}

Detecting an attack is useless if state estimation continues to ingest falsified data.
Once a bus is flagged ($y_{\mathrm{node},k} > \gamma_{\mathrm{loc}} = 0.5$), the digital twin isolates the tainted telemetry ($V_{k}, \theta_{k}, P_{k}, Q_{k}$).
Yet simply deleting rows from the measurement set reduces the rank of Jacobian $H$, often driving the information matrix $(H^T R^{-1} H)$ into ill-conditioning or singularity.
To maintain full algebraic observability, we synthesize replacement telemetry via admittance-weighted spatial interpolation from healthy neighboring buses:
\begin{equation}
z_{\mathrm{recon},k} = \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(k)} |Y_{kj}| \cdot z_{j}}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(k)} |Y_{kj}|} + \delta, \qquad \delta \sim \mathcal{U}(\pm 2\%)
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
where $|Y_{kj}|$ represents the line admittance magnitude and $\delta$ reflects normal sensor variance.
Because admittance weighting mirrors Kirchhoff's current conservation across incident branches, the reconstructed values align with the underlying power flow.
Injecting $z_{\mathrm{recon},k}$ preserves Jacobian rank, allowing WLS iterations to converge in under 2\,ms---well within typical 2--4\,s SCADA scan intervals.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Simulation Setup: Hospital Campus Microgrid}

Simulations were carried out using full AC Newton--Raphson power flow solutions in \texttt{pandapower}~\cite{thurner2018pandapower}.
We modeled a tertiary medical center using an IEEE 14-bus topology (14 buses, 20 lines, 217.0\,MW active load, 57.3\,MVAR reactive demand).
In this configuration, Bus~0 represents the main utility grid substation, while dedicated radial feeders power critical clinical facilities.
Most notably, Bus~5 supplies intensive care and operating theatres.
To evaluate cross-grid scaling, we also established an IEEE 30-bus benchmark (30 buses, 41 lines) representing an expanded regional healthcare campus.

\textbf{Synthetic Telemetry Synthesis.}
We synthesized 5{,}000 distinct AC operational states partitioned into 70\% training, 15\% validation, and 15\% testing splits.
Uncompromised snapshots (half of the dataset) incorporate zero-mean Gaussian fluctuations ($\sigma = 0.04$) across active and reactive bus injections.
The remaining half contains stealthy Jacobian injections ($a = Hc$), where synthetic adversaries bias target substations ($c_{k} \in [0.03, 0.08]$\,p.u.\ voltage offset, $c_{k+N} \in [0.05, 0.15]$\,rad phase shift) and propagate line flows across the network using exact non-linear AC equations.
Networks were trained across 60 epochs using Adam ($\mathrm{lr} = 0.005$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) over five random seeds.
Checkpoints, configuration files, and evaluation scripts are archived at \url{https://github.com/himanshu-2l/Graph-Digital-Twin-XAI-}.

Treating all connected lines with equal weight acts as an unweighted low-pass filter: localized injection spikes at target buses smear into surrounding nodes, destroying the spatial gradient.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Protecting hospital power networks demands defenses that combine topological adaptability with physical circuit fidelity.
By embedding branch admittances directly into GraphSAGE convolutions and supervising latent features with Integrated Gradients attribution penalties, the CGDT achieves $98.25\%$ detection F1 and $97.62\%$ bus localization F1 against stealthy AC false data injections.
Attribution supervision adds a 5.52\,pp localization boost while computing full feature explanations in 0.81\,ms.
Furthermore, automated sensor quarantine paired with admittance-weighted interpolation reduces post-attack WLS state estimation error by 99.97\%, maintaining full observability without tripping clinical loads.

\textbf{Clinical Operating Standards.}
Under IEC~60601-1 and IEEE~1547 requirements, prolonged voltage distortions risk disconnecting life-critical medical equipment.
With a 0.908\,ms inference latency and a 2\,ms total healing cycle, the CGDT executes comfortably within 2--4\,s SCADA sampling windows while local substation edge hosting ensures telemetry never crosses the OT perimeter.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.}
Subsequent research will transition to hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) testbeds to evaluate packet jitter and RTU telemetry latency.
Future extensions will also incorporate temporal graph models for multi-step campaigns and evaluate predictive estimators under multi-bus unobservability.
